About this document
This specification describes CRA Sentinel, the product security platform ABJ Experts is building for manufacturers who
sell connected products into the European Union. It is written for the people who will build it: engineers, architects, QA,
DevOps and product design. Everything here is meant to survive contact with a sprint board.

There is no commercial content in this document. No pricing, no revenue modelling, no sales strategy. Where a
capability exists because the business needs it, such as licence enforcement or usage counters, it is written up as a
system requirement and nothing more.


How to read the requirement identifiers
 Marker                What it means

 FR-XXX-000            A functional requirement inside a module. The middle segment names the module, for example FR-SBOM-014.

 NFR-000               A non-functional requirement. Performance, capacity, availability, install time.

 SEC-000               A security requirement.

 AI-000                A requirement on the AI subsystem.

A warning about the regulatory content

  Read this before you write any deadline logic
  Section 3 summarises the parts of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 that change how the software behaves, in particular the
  reporting clocks. Those provisions were checked against the European Commission and the published text of the regulation
  in July 2026. They are good enough to build against.
  They are not legal advice, and this document does not pretend otherwise. Before any customer uses the platform to file a
  real report with a regulator, the deadline arithmetic, the report content and the product classification rules need review by
  someone qualified in EU regulatory law or by a notified body.
  One practical consequence runs through the whole design. Regulatory timetables move. The EU AI Act had its high risk
  obligations pushed back by sixteen months during 2026. Every regulatory constant in this system is therefore configuration
  with an effective date, never a literal in code. See FR-RPT-008.

What we are building
CRA Sentinel is the system of record for product security in a manufacturing company. It answers four questions that a
manufacturer of connected products has to be able to answer on demand: what software is inside our products, which
of the known vulnerabilities in that software actually matter to us, what did we do about them, and can we prove all of it
to a regulator.

Today most mid-sized manufacturers answer those questions with a spreadsheet, a shared drive and one or two
engineers who happen to hold the knowledge in their heads. That works until a deadline measured in hours lands on
them.


Three properties that shape every requirement
Most of the difficult decisions in this document trace back to one of these three.

• The system runs on hard clocks. When an exploited vulnerability is confirmed, the first report is due in 24 hours.
   Timer correctness is a correctness problem on the same level as data integrity, not a notification nicety. Section 11 is
   devoted to it.
• Its output is evidence. Records live for a decade or more and may end up in front of a market surveillance authority.
   Immutability, provenance and a verifiable audit trail are functional requirements.
• It has to run inside the customer's perimeter. Manufacturers treat firmware and binaries as their most sensitive
   intellectual property. The same codebase has to work as multi-tenant SaaS and as an isolated installation with no
   internet access at all.

 CVE                The public identifier for a disclosed vulnerability.

 EPSS               Exploit Prediction Scoring System. Probability of exploitation in the next 30 days. Useful for ordering work, useless as a
                    compliance trigger.

 OSV                Open Source Vulnerabilities database. Gives precise affected version ranges keyed on PURL.

 SRP                Single Reporting Platform. The ENISA operated entry point for CRA reports.

3.1 A worked example, because the clocks confuse everyone
Take a real sequence. A manufacturer of an industrial gateway learns on Tuesday 14 April at 09:20 UTC that a
vulnerability in a library inside firmware 4.2 is being exploited in the wild. A mitigation config change is published on
Friday 24 April at 16:00 UTC. The permanent patch ships three weeks later.
  Tue 14 Apr 09:20 UTC   AWARENESS recorded by PSIRT lead. This is the anchor for stages 1 and 2.
        |
        | +24h
        v
  Wed 15 Apr 09:20 UTC   Stage 1 EARLY WARNING due. Art 14(2)(a)
        |                          Content: product identity, Member States, suspected malicious act.
        | +72h from AWARENESS (not from stage 1)
        v
  Fri 17 Apr 09:20 UTC   Stage 2 VULNERABILITY NOTIFICATION due. Art 14(2)(b)
        |
        |     ... stage 3 is still PENDING_ANCHOR here. No due date exists yet. ...
        |
  Fri 24 Apr 16:00 UTC   REMEDIATION AVAILABLE recorded. Mitigation counts. Patch not required.
        |                          THIS is the anchor for stage 3.
        | +14 days
        v
  Fri 08 May 16:00 UTC   Stage 3 FINAL REPORT due. Art 14(2)(c)
  Common wrong implementation: final report due 28 Apr (awareness + 14d) -> 10 days early, and
  it would have been impossible to complete because no remediation existed on that date.

Now the same incident classified as a severe incident instead. Stages 1 and 2 are identical. Stage 3 becomes one
calendar month after the stage 2 notification was actually submitted. If stage 2 went in on 17 April at 08:00, stage 3 is
due 17 May at 08:00. Calendar arithmetic, so 31 January plus one month lands on 28 or 29 February depending on the
year. There is a test for that in section 11.5.

  The design rule that falls out of this
  Every clock in the CRA has three independent properties: an anchor event, a duration, and a rule for when it stops. No two
  clocks share all three. So the timer engine is a generic obligation state machine driven by configuration rather than three
  hardcoded countdowns. Section 11 specifies it.

5.2 Architecture decision records

ADR-001 Modular monolith rather than microservices
Decision. One deployable NestJS application, organised into strictly bounded modules, plus a worker process built
from the same codebase. No independently deployed services.

                                                                                                                                         11
Why. This product ships as software a manufacturer's IT team has to run themselves. Every extra independently
deployed service multiplies install steps, upgrade coordination and support diagnosis for a customer who has no
platform team. A modular monolith keeps the option of extracting a service later without paying for that option now.

Cost and mitigation. Module boundaries decay unless something enforces them. Add an ESLint boundary rule so
cross module imports must go through a published module interface, and fail the build on violation. Without that rule this
becomes a tangle inside two quarters, and I have watched it happen on smaller codebases than this one.

ADR-002 NestJS talks to Postgres directly, not through PostgREST
Decision. The API connects to Supabase Postgres over a normal Postgres connection using a dedicated restricted
role. Domain queries do not go through the Supabase auto generated REST layer, and the browser does not query
Supabase directly for domain tables.

Why. Three reasons. Authorisation has to live in one place, and splitting it between RLS via PostgREST and guards in
NestJS produces gaps that are very hard to test for. The domain has multi table operations, such as SBOM diffing and
timer state transitions, that are awkward through a generic REST layer. And the audit ledger needs every mutation to
carry an actor, a reason and a correlation id, which a generic CRUD layer cannot enforce.

Cost and mitigation. We lose instant CRUD and write endpoints by hand. Accepted. The browser may use the
Supabase client for exactly two things: the authentication handshake, and uploading or downloading files through short
lived signed URLs that our API issues. Everything else is blocked, and we enforce that by revoking anon role privileges
on domain tables rather than trusting convention.

ADR-003 Drizzle ORM, not Prisma
Decision. Drizzle with node-postgres for typed queries. Plain SQL migration files applied through the Supabase CLI
migration mechanism.

Why. Supavisor in transaction pooling mode does not support session level prepared statements, which is a recurring
source of friction with Prisma. More important, the tenancy model in ADR-005 needs a SET LOCAL executed inside the
same transaction as the query, and Drizzle exposes that cleanly. Drizzle also emits SQL close enough to hand written
that RLS behaviour stays predictable when you read it.

Cost and mitigation. Less scaffolding, more explicit SQL. We were going to write SQL anyway, because RLS policies,
triggers, functions and extensions cannot be expressed in an ORM schema language. Keep one migration pipeline so
cloud and self hosted apply the same ordered set.

ADR-004 Supabase Auth behind a mandatory abstraction

  This is the highest risk decision in the stack
  Supabase Auth fits the SaaS tier and early customers well. It has specific gaps against enterprise and on premises
  requirements, verified in July 2026:
  There is no SCIM provisioning. Enterprise buyers routinely require automated user lifecycle from Entra ID or Okta, so we
  implement SCIM 2.0 ourselves in NestJS.
  Single Logout is not supported. Signing out of our application does not end the IdP session, and some corporate security
  policies require that it does.
  Enterprise SSO is SAML 2.0 only, so a customer standardised on enterprise OIDC federation may not be servable without
  changes.
  Self hosted SAML is not turnkey. It needs GOTRUE_SAML_ENABLED set, a PKCS#1 private key generated and injected
  base64 encoded, API_EXTERNAL_URL pointing at a reachable URL containing /auth/v1, and open Kong routes added for
  the ACS and metadata endpoints. Self hosted Supabase is community supported.


Decision. Use Supabase Auth for MVP and V1. Put every call to it behind an internal IdentityProvider interface with
methods such as authenticate, getSession, revokeSession, linkSsoIdentity and provisionUser. No file outside the single
adapter may import the Supabase auth client.

                                                                                                                         12
Why. It keeps MVP velocity while making a later swap to Keycloak, Zitadel or Ory a contained piece of work instead of a
rewrite. All three are self hostable and all three cover OIDC federation, SLO and richer session policy. Given that on
premises enterprise is central to this product, the odds of needing that swap are high enough to plan for.

Cost and mitigation. One layer of indirection. Write a conformance test suite against the interface early so a second
implementation can be validated in days. Roles and permissions stay in application tables and are never derived from
JWT claims alone, so the swap never touches authorisation.

ADR-005 Shared schema tenancy with two independent isolation layers
Decision. One schema for all tenants. Every tenant scoped table carries a non-nullable organisation_id. Isolation is
enforced twice, by Postgres RLS keyed on a transaction local setting and by an application tenant guard. Neither layer
is trusted on its own.

Why. Schema per tenant or database per tenant makes migrations, platform operations and connection pooling
considerably harder. Customers who genuinely need physical separation get it through the private cloud and on
premises deployment modes, so physical isolation is a deployment choice rather than a data model choice.

Cost and mitigation. RLS correctness becomes a first class test concern, covered in section 6.4. The application
database role must not be the Supabase service_role and must not have BYPASSRLS, or the entire isolation layer
disappears silently while every test still passes.

ADR-006 BullMQ on Redis
Decision. BullMQ backed by Redis for all background processing, including timer notifications.

Why. The workload needs delayed jobs, repeatable schedules, per tenant concurrency caps, priorities, retry with
backoff and dead letter handling. BullMQ has all of it natively. A database backed queue such as pgmq would save one
container on premises but lacks the scheduling and rate limiting behaviour the ingestion and AI pipelines depend on.

Cost and mitigation. One more container in every deployment including the smallest. Redis is explicitly not a source of
truth. Every durable obligation, timer and job intent is written to Postgres first and the queue only delivers. A
reconciliation job rebuilds queue state from the database, so losing Redis entirely degrades how quickly people get told,
never whether a deadline exists.

ADR-007 TypeScript everywhere until reachability arrives
Decision. SBOM parsing, matching and enrichment are TypeScript in the worker tier for MVP and V1. A separate
Python analysis service appears only when call graph reachability is built in V2.

Why. The MVP workload is parsing, normalisation, set operations and version range comparison. TypeScript handles
all of it and there are maintained libraries for CycloneDX and SPDX. Adding a second language runtime before it earns
its place doubles the build, test, packaging and on premises deployment surface. Reachability analysis genuinely needs
the Python and native ecosystem, so that is the honest trigger point.

Cost and mitigation. Put the matcher behind a clean internal interface now so the V2 service slots in as an alternative
implementation rather than forcing a refactor.

ADR-008 Postgres full text search and pgvector, no separate search cluster
Decision. Postgres tsvector search with pg_trgm for fuzzy matching, pgvector for embeddings. No OpenSearch, no
Elasticsearch.

Why. Search volumes here are per tenant and modest: evidence documents, components, advisories, audit entries.
Postgres handles that comfortably at our expected scale. Every component we avoid is real relief for on premises
operators, and putting a JVM search cluster into an air gapped manufacturing site is a support liability nobody thanks
you for.

Cost and mitigation. Revisit at V2 with an explicit trigger rather than a vague intention. If p95 audit search latency
passes 1.5 seconds on a tenant holding more than 50 million audit rows, open the question again.

                                                                                                                      13
ADR-009 Provider agnostic AI gateway with a real local option
Decision. All inference goes through an internal gateway exposing a narrow interface: complete, embed, extract.
Providers are pluggable, either a cloud API in an EU region or a local model served by Ollama or vLLM. Selection is per
tenant configuration.

Why. A meaningful share of target customers will refuse any external model call that touches product data. Local
inference has to be a tested first class path rather than a bolt on. The gateway is also the only sensible place to put
injection filtering, token accounting, prompt versioning and the inference audit trail.

Cost and mitigation. Every AI feature has to degrade acceptably on a smaller local model. Prompts are versioned and
evaluated against both a frontier cloud model and a representative local model before release. If a feature only works on
the largest models, it does not ship.

ADR-010 Deterministic core, AI on top as an advisory layer
Decision. Component matching, version range evaluation, deadline computation, retention calculation and control
mapping are deterministic code. AI never produces those values. AI explains, drafts, classifies, extracts and ranks,
always as a suggestion carrying provenance, always subject to a human accepting it before it becomes part of an
evidence record.

Why. Output from this system may end up in front of a regulator. An answer nobody can reproduce is not evidence. This
boundary is also what makes the product hard to copy, because the difficult engineering sits in the correlation layer
rather than in prompt wording.

Cost and mitigation. Every AI produced field carries source equal to ai, a model identifier, a prompt version, and
accepted_by with accepted_at that stay null until a human acts. Reports and exports have to show accepted AI assisted
content differently from human authored content.

ADR-011 One codebase across every deployment mode
Decision. SaaS, private cloud, on premises and air gapped all run identical container images. Behaviour varies only
through configuration and licence gated feature flags. There is no on premises fork.

Why. A fork drifts, and drift in a compliance product means a customer's evidence behaves differently from anything we
tested. It also multiplies security patching across variants.

Cost and mitigation. Every external dependency needs an offline path: vulnerability feeds, AI models, licence
validation, telemetry. Anything that cannot work offline is feature flagged and degrades rather than crashing. CI runs a
full stack smoke test with egress blocked.

ADR-012 Append only audit ledger with hash chaining
Decision. Audit events go to an append only table. No application role holds UPDATE or DELETE on it. Each row
stores a SHA-256 of its canonical content plus the previous row's hash, forming a chain per organisation.

Why. An audit trail is only worth what its tamper evidence is worth. Chaining lets a customer or an auditor verify integrity
without trusting us, which turns out to matter in evidence led sales, and it costs very little to implement.

Cost and mitigation. Audit writes must not sit inside a long running transaction that would block the chain head. Use a
per tenant advisory lock for the head update, and expose a verification endpoint that walks a range and reports breaks.
FR-AUD-011.

ADR-013 Storage abstraction over the S3 API only
Decision. All binary persistence goes through one internal storage interface implemented against the S3 API.
Supabase Storage in cloud deployments, MinIO or a customer bucket on premises.

Why. It keeps a single code path for signed URLs, lifecycle rules, server side encryption and multipart upload, and it
means an on premises customer can point us at storage they already run.


                                                                                                                         14
Cost and mitigation. We give up provider specific conveniences. Fine. Write an integration test suite that runs against
both MinIO and Supabase Storage in CI so divergence surfaces early.

ADR-014 Expand then contract for every schema change
Decision. Migrations are forward only and backward compatible across at least one release. A column is added and
populated in one release, and only dropped or renamed in a later one.

Why. SaaS needs rolling deploys where two versions run at once. On premises customers skip versions and roll back
when an upgrade goes wrong at 22:00 on a Friday. Both require that the previous application version can still read the
new schema.

Cost and mitigation. More migration steps and some temporary duplication. Add a CI job that restores the previous
release, applies the new migrations, and runs the previous release's test suite against the migrated database.

6.2 Row level security
Every tenant scoped table has RLS enabled and forced. Policies read a transaction local setting that the application
sets on each request.
 -- applied to every tenant scoped table through a migration helper
 ALTER TABLE finding ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;
 ALTER TABLE finding FORCE           ROW LEVEL SECURITY;

 CREATE POLICY tenant_isolation ON finding
    USING           (organisation_id = current_setting('app.organisation_id', true)::uuid)
    WITH CHECK (organisation_id = current_setting('app.organisation_id', true)::uuid);

 -- the application role. NOT service_role. NOT a superuser. No BYPASSRLS.
 CREATE ROLE cras_app NOINHERIT LOGIN;
 GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE ON ALL TABLES IN SCHEMA public TO cras_app;
 REVOKE UPDATE, DELETE ON audit_event FROM cras_app;                           -- ADR-012
 REVOKE INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE ON cve, kev_entry, epss_score, osv_advisory FROM cras_app;


  The most dangerous mistake available in this codebase
  Connecting NestJS with the Supabase service_role key, or any role holding BYPASSRLS, silently switches off every
  isolation policy. Nothing breaks. Every test still passes, because the application guard is filtering correctly on its own. A
  cross tenant leak would then wait until production to introduce itself.
  Defend against it mechanically. On boot, run a query that must return zero rows under RLS with no tenant context set, and
  refuse to start the process if it returns anything. SEC-014.

6.3 How tenant context travels
Boundary                Mechanism

HTTP request            TenantContextMiddleware resolves the organisation from the authenticated principal's active membership. Never
                        from a URL segment or a request body, because those are attacker controlled. Held in AsyncLocalStorage for the
                        request.

Database transaction    A Drizzle transaction wrapper issues set_config('app.organisation_id', $1, true) as the first statement. The trailing
                        true makes it transaction local, so a pooled connection cannot carry context into the next borrower.

Queue job               Every payload carries organisationId, actorId and correlationId. The job wrapper re-establishes context before the
                        handler runs. A job with no tenant id is rejected and dead lettered rather than guessed at.

AI inference            The gateway receives tenant context and puts the tenant predicate inside the vector query itself. Cross tenant
                        retrieval is structurally impossible rather than filtered out afterwards.


 // mandatory wrapper. A lint rule bans direct db.transaction() calls outside this file.
 async function withTenant<T>(orgId: string, fn: (tx: Tx) => Promise<T>): Promise<T> {
     return db.transaction(async (tx) => {
      await tx.execute(sql`SELECT set_config('app.organisation_id', ${orgId}, true)`);
      return fn(tx);
     });
 }

7.2 Authorisation model
Role based, with attribute rules layered on top. Roles hold permissions, permissions are verbs on resource types, and
attribute rules narrow which instances a principal can touch.

Permission strings follow the pattern resource:action, for example finding:triage, report:submit or evidence:delete. The
whole set lives in one shared TypeScript constant so the frontend and backend cannot fall out of step.

• Product scoping. A principal can be restricted to a subset of products. Applied as a predicate in the data layer,
  never as a filter in the UI.
• Separation of duties. Whoever drafts a regulatory report cannot approve its submission, unless the organisation
  explicitly turns that control off and accepts a recorded audit exception. FR-RPT-022.
• External scoping. Suppliers and auditors start with access to nothing and receive an explicit allow list of records.

Data model

8.1 Conventions
• Primary keys are UUID v7 where the library allows it, because time ordered keys index far better than v4. Generated
  by the application, never accepted from a client.
• Every tenant scoped table carries organisation_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES organisation(id).
• Every table carries created_at, updated_at, created_by and updated_by.
• Soft delete through deleted_at on user facing entities. Evidence and audit rows are never soft deleted while a
  retention obligation is live.
• All timestamps are timestamptz in UTC. Never plain timestamp. Clock arithmetic happens in UTC and only the
  presentation layer converts.
• Version strings are compared with ecosystem specific semantics. String ordering on versions is a bug, not a shortcut.
• Enumerations are text with a CHECK constraint rather than native Postgres enums, because altering a native enum
  inside a migration is unpleasant.
• Money does not appear in this system at all, which removes a whole class of rounding problem.

8.4 State machines
Three entities have state machines worth writing down, because ad hoc status fields are where inconsistent data comes
from.
 FINDING
   open ---> in_triage ---> awaiting_approval ---> closed
     ^          |                  |                 |
     |          v                  v (rejected)      | (component reappears in a later release)
     |      suppressed ---------> in_triage          |
     |          | (expiry reached)                   |
     +----------+------------------------------------+    ---> reopened
 PRODUCT LIFECYCLE
   development ---> placed_on_market ---> in_support ---> end_of_support ---> withdrawn
                       |                                                          ^
                       +----------------------------------------------------------+
   placed_on_market requires a date. That date starts the 10 year retention clock and cannot
   be cleared afterwards without a recorded reason.
 OBLIGATION STAGE
   pending_anchor ---> running ---> submitted
                          |
                          +-------> overdue ---> submitted (late)
   overdue is permanent and auditable. A later submission is recorded next to it, never instead of it.

The matching engine
This is the part of the system that decides whether anyone trusts the product. It takes a normalised component and
works out which advisories genuinely apply to it. Everything downstream, the queue, the priority score, the obligations,
the technical file, rests on this being right.


10.1 Why the obvious approach fails
The naive implementation matches component names against text in CVE descriptions. It produces thousands of
matches per product, most of them wrong, and analysts stop opening the tool within a fortnight. The second naive
implementation matches on CPE alone. CPE strings are inconsistently assigned, frequently too broad, and often
describe a product family rather than a specific version, so a single CPE match can implicate a component that was
never affected.

The approach that works is a layered one. Try the precise key first, fall back to the loose key only where the precise one
is unavailable, and record which path produced the result so a human can interrogate it.


10.2 Algorithm
 for each component in release:

   # Layer 1   PURL exact and range matching       (primary path, highest confidence)
   if component.purl exists:
        advisories = osv.lookup(purl_type, namespace, name)
        for advisory in advisories:
            for range in advisory.affected_ranges where range.ecosystem == component.ecosystem:
                if compare(component.version, range, ecosystem_comparator) == AFFECTED:
                     emit finding(method = purl_range, confidence = 0.95)
        continue            # do NOT also run CPE matching. That is where duplicates come from.

   # Layer 2   CPE matching                        (fallback only, lower confidence)
   if component.cpe exists:
        candidates = nvd.lookup_by_cpe(component.cpe)
        for cve in candidates:
            verdict = evaluate_cpe_node_tree(cve.configurations, component.cpe, component.version)
            if verdict == AFFECTED:
                conf = 0.70 if cpe_is_version_specific else 0.45
                emit finding(method = cpe_match, confidence = conf)

   # Layer 3   Heuristic name and version          (V2, suggestion only, never auto-opened)
   if no purl and no cpe:
        emit low_confidence_candidate for analyst confirmation, flagged as unverified

 post-pass:
   deduplicate by (component, advisory_id) keeping the highest confidence method
   carry forward any existing human assessment for the same (component_version, advisory_id)
   recompute priority_score with the current formula version

The obligation timer engine

  Design rule
  The database is the only source of truth for deadlines. The queue is a notification transport and nothing more. If Redis is
  wiped, deadlines are still correct once the reconciliation job runs, and only notification timeliness suffers. Any design where a
  deadline exists solely as a delayed Redis job is rejected at review.



11.1 Mechanics
1. Creating an obligation writes one obligation_stage row per required stage, each carrying its anchor_event,
    duration_interval and state.
2. A stage whose anchor timestamp is known computes due_at and moves to running. A stage whose anchor has not
    happened stays in pending_anchor with a null due_at, which is why the final report stage shows no date until
    remediation exists.
3. Recording an anchor event, whether awareness_at, notification submission or remediation_available_at,
    recomputes the dependent stage due_at inside the same transaction.
4. obligation.tick runs every minute, reads all running stages, and emits any threshold notification not yet emitted.
    Emission is recorded, so notifications never duplicate.
5. Delayed obligation.notify jobs give low latency alerts near a deadline, and always re-read state from the database
    before sending, because the situation may have changed since the job was queued.
6. A stage past due_at with no submission becomes overdue. That is a permanent auditable fact. A later submission is
    recorded alongside it and never erases it.


11.2 Configuration shape
 obligation_rule_set:
   effective_from: 2026-09-11T00:00:00Z
   effective_to:       null
   jurisdiction:       EU-CRA
   types:
      actively_exploited_vulnerability:
        stages:
           - stage: early_warning         anchor: awareness                      duration: PT24H
           - stage: notification          anchor: awareness                      duration: PT72H
           - stage: final_report          anchor: remediation_available          duration: P14D
      severe_incident:
        stages:
           - stage: early_warning         anchor: awareness                      duration: PT24H
           - stage: notification          anchor: awareness                      duration: PT72H
           - stage: final_report          anchor: notification_submitted         duration: P1M
   thresholds: [0.50, 0.75, 0.90, 1.00]

An obligation stores the rule set version it was created under. If the regulation changes, new obligations pick up the new
rule set and existing ones keep the rules that applied when they started. That behaviour is what makes an amendment a
configuration change rather than a migration project.

AI subsystem

12.1 Rules that are not negotiable

 Read these before writing a single prompt
 AI never computes a fact the system can derive. Deadlines, version matches, retention dates and coverage percentages are
 deterministic code, and no AI touches those paths.
 Every output is a suggestion until a human accepts it. The database stores the suggestion, the model, the prompt version
 and the accepting user. Nothing enters an evidence record unaccepted.
 Every output carries provenance. A drafted paragraph links to the records that produced it, and an assertion with no
 traceable source is treated as a defect rather than a stylistic quirk.
 Every feature has to work on a locally hosted model, because a real share of customers run air gapped. A feature that only
 works on a frontier model does not ship.
 Retrieval is tenant scoped inside the query, not filtered afterwards.
 Customer data is never used for training, by us or by any configured provider. That has to be verified per provider and stated
 in the trust documentation.

 Aspect                      Specification

 Problem                     An analyst opens a queue of several hundred findings and cannot tell quickly which ones deserve attention or

13.1 Conventions
Aspect                       Convention

Style                        REST over HTTPS with JSON. OpenAPI 3.1 generated from NestJS
                             decorators and published as a build artefact.

Versioning                   URI prefix /api/v1. Breaking changes need a new major version, and
                             both run in parallel for a documented deprecation window.

Filtering                    Explicit typed query parameters validated with Zod. No generic query
                             language passthrough, which would become an unbounded attack
                             surface.

Errors                       RFC 9457 Problem Details. Every error carries a stable machine
                             readable type, a human readable title and a correlationId that
                             matches the server log.

Not found versus forbidden   Requesting a resource in another tenant returns 404, never 403. A
                             403 confirms the resource exists, which is itself a leak.

Idempotency                  All unsafe methods accept an Idempotency-Key header. It is
                             mandatory for report submission, SBOM upload and bulk triage.

Concurrency                  Optimistic locking through a version field on mutable entities. A stale
                             write returns 409 with current state so the client can show a diff
                             instead of losing work.

Rate limiting                Per principal and per organisation with RateLimit response headers.
                             Ingestion endpoints get their own higher limits.

Long operations              Return 202 with a job resource URL. Progress is polled or streamed
                             over Server-Sent Events. No request blocks on long work.

Time                         ISO 8601 with an explicit UTC offset, always. Durations as ISO 8601
                             durations.

Field naming                 camelCase in JSON, snake_case in the database, mapped in exactly
                             one place.

20.2 Self-hosted Supabase, verified constraints

 Plan for this now, not during the first enterprise deal
 Self hosted Supabase is community supported, and several things that are turnkey on Supabase Cloud need manual work
 when you host it yourself.
 SAML SSO needs GOTRUE_SAML_ENABLED set to true, an RSA private key generated in PKCS#1 format and supplied
 base64 encoded through GOTRUE_SAML_PRIVATE_KEY, API_EXTERNAL_URL pointing at a publicly reachable URL
 containing /auth/v1, and unauthenticated Kong routes added for the SAML ACS and metadata endpoints. Identity providers
 are registered at runtime through the Auth admin API rather than through environment variables.
 Single Logout is not supported by Supabase Auth. Document that plainly for customers with an SLO policy rather than
 letting it surface during a security review.
 SCIM is not provided at all, so we implement it.
 Kubernetes deployment relies on community Helm charts rather than an official distribution. Pin and vendor them and do not
 track upstream automatically.
 The action that follows: build the whole self hosted configuration, environment, Kong routes, keys and migrations, as version
 controlled templated infrastructure from day one. Treating on premises as a packaging exercise to do later is how it ends up
 taking a quarter. The ADR-004 abstraction exists so that replacing GoTrue with Keycloak or Zitadel stays a contained
 change if these gaps block a deal.

Data lifecycle and retention

 The genuine conflict at the centre of this system
 The CRA wants technical documentation kept for at least ten years. Data protection law gives people the right to have their
 personal data erased. Those two collide whenever an evidence record contains personal data, which in practice means the
 names, signatures and contact details of engineers, approvers and supplier staff.
 Our design response is to keep personal identity data physically separate from evidence content. Evidence references a
 person by a stable internal identifier. When an erasure request arrives, the identity record is pseudonymised while the
 evidence, its audit trail and the identifier stay intact and verifiable. The retention obligation is met and the personal data is
 gone.
 This is an engineering design anticipating a legal requirement. It is not a legal conclusion, and it needs validation with
 counsel before launch. Flagged again in section 27.

FR-RET-001        MUST      Retention computed per product as the later of placed on market plus 10 years and support period end,

                            obligation doing the blocking.

                            pseudonymisation does not destroy evidence integrity.

Delivery phases and build order
Phases are defined by capability rather than by date. Each has an exit condition that has to be met before the next
starts. The order is deliberate: the correlation engine and the timer engine are the two hardest correctness problems in
the product and both are front loaded, because discovering a problem in either one late is expensive.


MVP prove the correlation engine
Goal. A design partner uploads a real SBOM and gets back a finding set they consider accurate enough to act on.

• Organisation, users, roles, MFA. FR-ORG-001 and 002, FR-AUTH-001 and 002, FR-IAM-001 to 004.
• Product and release registry. FR-PROD-001 and 002.
• SBOM ingestion, validation, normalisation. FR-SBOM-001 to 004, 007, 015.
• Feed mirroring and PURL first matching. FR-VULN-001, 002, 004, 006, 007, 009, 011.
• Triage queue, finding detail, VEX assessment. FR-TRI-001 to 005.
• Evidence upload. FR-EVD-001 and 003.
• Audit trail. FR-AUD-001 and 002.
• Dashboard FR-AN-001 and email notification FR-WF-005.
• SaaS plus single host Compose deployment.

  MVP exit condition
  False positive rate on the golden dataset below the agreed threshold, and at least three design partners confirming the
  finding set is actionable. If matching accuracy is not there, do not move on. Everything downstream assumes analysts trust
  the queue, and no amount of good design compensates for a queue full of noise.



V1 the first commercially deployable release
Goal. A customer runs a complete regulatory lifecycle end to end without leaving the product.

• The full reporting obligation and timer engine. All FR-RPT and all FR-SLA. This is what defines the phase.
• Technical file and declaration of conformity. FR-TF-001 to 016.
• CPE matching, continuous re-evaluation, priority scoring. FR-VULN-005, 008, 010, 014, 015.
• Triage approvals, bulk actions, propagation, VEX generation. FR-TRI-006 to 015.
• Evidence library complete. FR-EVD-002, 004 to 011, 014 to 018.
• Supplier portal. FR-SUP-001 to 007.
• Framework packs and control library. FR-FRM-001 to 003 and 006.
• SAML SSO, product scoping, separation of duties. FR-AUTH-003, 004, 008, 010, 011 and FR-IAM-005 to 007, 010.
• Integrations: GitHub, GitLab, Jira, Slack, Teams, SMTP.
• AI-1, AI-2, AI-3 and AI-6 with the full gateway, guardrails and evaluation harness.
• Licensing, system health, diagnostics. FR-ADM-001 to 007.
• Private cloud and Kubernetes on premises deployment.
Exit condition. A customer completes a full rehearsed obligation lifecycle including a signed manual submission export,
and the timer test matrix passes in its entirety with no skipped cases.

• Air gapped deployment, offline feed bundles, offline updates. FR-VULN-003, FR-DEP-007, FR-ADM-010.

27.2 Assumptions that still need validating
1. Target customers can produce SBOMs from their build pipelines, or will accept help doing it. If they cannot,
   ingestion is the wrong front door and the product shape changes. Validate this with design partners before anything
   else, because it is the most consequential open question in the document.
2. PURL coverage in real customer SBOMs is high enough that PURL first matching carries most findings. Embedded
   and firmware SBOMs may skew heavily towards CPE, which would move CPE matching from V1 into MVP.
3. Customers accept a human in the loop AI model rather than expecting the tool to triage on its own.
4. The ENISA SRP will expose a machine accessible submission interface. We deliberately do not assume this in V1.
5. Local model quality is good enough for AI-1 through AI-3 at the 8B class. That has to be measured on the golden
   set, not assumed because it feels likely.
6. Mid-market customers will run a self hosted deployment using the documentation we provide. NFR-017 is the proxy
   metric. If we cannot hit it, managed private cloud becomes the default answer for on premises rather than customer
   managed.


27.3 Open questions for the team
1. Do we support a customer supplied Postgres in on premises mode, or mandate the bundled Supabase stack? This
   changes the support burden materially and should be settled before V1 packaging work starts.
2. Is the supplier portal a route group inside the main application or a separately deployed surface? Separate
   deployment reduces blast radius, shared deployment reduces effort. Both are defensible.

3. What is the minimum viable framework pack for MVP: CRA Annex I on its own, or CRA plus one crosswalk to
    demonstrate the reuse argument?
4. Do we build CI plugins ourselves, or start with a documented API and worked examples? Plugins help adoption and

5. How is the curated crosswalk authored and maintained, through in-product editorial tooling or a versioned content

6. Does the audit hash chain need external anchoring, such as a periodically published digest, to convince an auditor,
